\begin{abstract}
Reasoning-augmented language models are deployed by default for high-stakes
queries, and how long a model ``thinks'' is now a user-facing knob. The
prevailing assumption is that more thinking yields more trustworthy answers.
We test it. We formalize the capability--reliability trade-off as a measurable
\emph{reliability tax}, and ask whether it is a law---and whether it is
\emph{predictable}. A black-box budget sweep over four task types measures
accuracy and three reliability axes jointly (calibration, selective
prediction, abstention-versus-fabrication). A first real run already shows the
tax is \emph{task-dependent} and \emph{switch-triggered}: on false-premise
items, enabling thinking raises fabrication from 3.8\% to 21.3\%, while
mathematical reasoning is unaffected.
\end{abstract}

\section{Background \& Motivation}
Reasoning-augmented language models (LRMs) spend a variable amount of compute
``thinking'' before answering \citep{Guo2025DeepSeekR1}, and this budget is now
a user-facing control \citep{Muennighoff2025s1,Snell2024TestTimeCompute}. The
implicit contract is simple: \emph{more thinking buys more trustworthy
answers}. Practitioners act on it---raising the budget to ``max'' whenever an
answer matters. Our survey (below) finds that contract contradicted across the
field. If ``think longer'' is not a safe default, the budget must be chosen per
query---which requires knowing whether the trade-off is \emph{predictable}.

\section{Research Questions \& Hypotheses}
\textbf{RQ.} Does increasing a model's thinking budget systematically trade
reliability for accuracy, and can the degradation be foreseen?
\textbf{H1 (task-dependence):} the ratio of reliability loss to accuracy gain
differs across task types by at least $2\times$. \textbf{H2 (predictability,
primary):} a predictor trained on a held-out half decides, at low budget and
\emph{without} seeing the high-budget answer, whether continuing will help, and
the induced policy beats the best fixed budget in \emph{usable accuracy} at
matched cost. \textbf{H3 (negative control):} shuffling the trace features
removes H2's advantage---if not, the predictor exploits an artefact.
Each claim carries an explicit falsification condition, on a held-out prompt
split.

\section{Survey \& Preliminary Literature}
We surveyed the ACL~2026 landscape (1{,}409 papers) to locate the gap, and
found the same warning recurring across otherwise unrelated subfields:
capability gains are bought with reliability losses. Reinforcement learning
that raises accuracy degrades calibration \citep{Wang2026CAPO,Jin2025Entropy};
reasoning enhancement amplifies tool hallucination, method-agnostically
\citep{Yin2025ReasoningTrap}; ``slower'' models are less truthful under
misleading input \citep{Fang2025Slower}; reasoning models stay confidently
wrong on false premises \citep{Sathyanathan2025Presuppositions} and are
\emph{worse} at abstaining on unanswerable questions
\citep{Kirichenko2025AbstentionBench}; and they spend an order of magnitude more
tokens while sometimes being \emph{less} accurate
\citep{Srivastava2025Overthink,Shojaee2025IllusionThinking}.

This evidence is fragmented along three axes. It is \emph{task-local} (one
benchmark, often one model or recipe); \emph{training-side} (the sharpest
results concern what RLVR does to weights \citep{Yue2025RLBeyondBase,
Zhao2025SpuriousRewards}, which API users cannot reach); and
\emph{single-axis}---calibration \emph{or} hallucination \emph{or} abstention,
never measured jointly, so we cannot tell whether the failures share a cause.
The two closest studies vary the budget and measure one axis:
\citet{DontThinkTwice2025} show over-reasoning impairs calibration;
\citet{WhenMoreThinkingHurts2026} characterize its diminishing---sometimes
negative---marginal utility. Both are \emph{descriptive}: neither asks whether
the losses across axes \emph{co-move}, nor whether they are \emph{predictable}
before a budget is committed. We therefore change the question from
\emph{whether} the trade-off exists to \emph{whether it is a law}---and whether
it can be turned into a deployment rule.

\section{Method \& Experimental Design}
\paragraph{The object and the tax.} Everything is read off the
\emph{budget--reliability profile}---accuracy and each reliability axis as a
function of the thinking budget, reported against \emph{realized} reasoning
tokens since nominal knobs are not comparable across providers. With every score
oriented so that higher is better,
$\tau_r=(\partial\rho_r/\partial b)/(\partial \mathrm{acc}/\partial b)$ is the
reliability given up per unit of accuracy gained; because $\tau_r$ diverges
where accuracy saturates, our primary summaries are bounded instead---the
fixed-coverage risk $R(c)$ and the area under the risk--coverage curve
\citep{Geifman2017Selective}.

\paragraph{Three axes.} (i) \emph{Calibration}: ECE \citep{Guo2017ECE} and
verbalized overconfidence
\citep{Kadavath2022KnowWhatTheyKnow,Tian2023JustAsk,Xiong2023Confidence}.
(ii) \emph{Selective prediction}: $R(c)$ and AURC
\citep{Geifman2017Selective,Slobodkin2024Abstention}. (iii)
\emph{Abstention vs.\ fabrication} on false-premise items, classified as
refute / hedge / endorse, with fabrication rate = endorse share
\citep{Sathyanathan2025Presuppositions,Kirichenko2025AbstentionBench}.

\paragraph{Test sets.} Four task types span where extra compute should help and
where it can hurt, each with a verifiable gold standard:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt
  \item \textbf{Math reasoning} (GSM8K, MATH-500; numeric match) --- the
  \emph{positive control}, where thinking should help.
  \item \textbf{Factual QA} (Natural Questions; alias match) --- closed-book
  recall, where thinking should mostly \emph{waste} budget.
  \item \textbf{Multi-hop QA} (HotpotQA; alias match) --- answers requiring
  composition of $\ge\!2$ facts; the reasoning test, where errors cascade.
  \item \textbf{False premise} --- SciFact claims whose evidence
  \emph{contradicts} them \citep{Wadden2020SciFact}, rewritten as questions
  that presuppose them. The only setting where the reliable behaviour is to
  \emph{refuse}; our \emph{negative control} and the sole source of the
  fabrication axis (classified refute / hedge / endorse).
\end{itemize}
200 items per task, with a prompt-level split held out for evaluating the
stopping rule.

\paragraph{Budget and sampling.} The budget axis uses the provider's official
controls---thinking on/off and reasoning-effort levels---rather than
\texttt{max\_tokens}, which would confound budget with truncation; realized
reasoning tokens are logged per response. Four budgets $\times$ two prompt
templates $\times\,k{=}4$ samples per item, with prompt-clustered bootstrap
intervals \citep{Zhang2025BeyondSingular,Miller2024ErrorBars}.

\paragraph{From profile to decision.} The profile yields an oracle: the budget
maximizing \emph{usable accuracy}, i.e.\ accuracy at the coverage where ECE
stays below a target $\epsilon$. We then test whether that oracle is
\emph{findable} from low-budget features alone (realized length, verbalized
confidence, self-consistency \citep{Manakul2023SelfCheckGPT}, hedging markers),
with the H3 permutation control.

\section{Preliminary Result}
A first real run on \texttt{deepseek-flash} (1{,}280 calls, 0 errors,
${\sim}$\textyen7--13) shows the tax is \emph{task-dependent} and
\emph{switch-triggered}:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{lcccc}
\hline
Task & Acc.\ off$\to$max & ECE off$\to$max & Fabrication off$\to$max \\
\hline
Math          & .988$\to$.950 & .01$\to$.04 & --- \\
Factual QA    & .475$\to$.550 & .45$\to$.39 & --- \\
Multi-hop QA  & .463$\to$.562 & .46$\to$.41 & --- \\
False-premise & .962$\to$.800 & .03$\to$.13 & \textbf{.038$\to$.213} \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
On false-premise items---where there is \emph{no} accuracy to gain---enabling
thinking multiplies fabrication by 5.6$\times$ and breaks calibration, while
mathematical reasoning is untouched. Notably the effect is a \emph{step} at the
thinking on/off boundary rather than a smooth function of effort level, which
sharpens H1 into a stronger, more falsifiable claim.

\textbf{Outcomes.} (1)~A cross-task, cross-family test of whether the
trade-off is a law, and where it is worst; (2)~a held-out, cost-matched
evaluation of an oracle-free \emph{stopping rule}; (3)~a released protocol,
prompts and responses.
